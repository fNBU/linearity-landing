Objective

To learn how to employ and work with vectors and matrices in finite dimensional vector spaces (\(\mathbb R^2, \mathbb R^3, \mathbb R^n\)) and abstract (including infinite dimensional) vector spaces.

Goals

Your final grade will be determined by the extent to which you

  1. (further) develop spatial reasoning based on ideas of vectors and dot-products,

  2. become fluent in solving linear systems of equations with or without parameters, and gain an understanding of what the solutions represent geometrically,

  3. become fluent in matrix algebra and determinants,

  4. develop an ability to work with concepts of determinant, rank, nullity, eigenvalues and eigenvectors, both computationally and geometrically, and

  5. get an idea of abstract vector spaces and ways in which to represent abstract linear algebra in coordinates; develop fluency in changing bases and coordinates.

Grading

Your final letter grade is based on the evidence I have that you accomplished the goals in the previous section. To help me make this assessment, I will assign and record grades for the following types of work:

  • weekly homework assignments, due the first class day of the week, starting in week 2
  • weekly quizzes, at the beginning of the second class day of the week, starting in week 2
  • one or two midterm exams
  • a final exam

More information on the format of these and their grading can be found below (Assessments).

Assessments

Here are details on the three kinds of assessments that will be assigned.

Homework

Homework exercises will be assigned weekly from the course notes.

Not all problems will be assigned. If you want more practice, you can do the unassigned problems or ask for recommended problems from the other resources.

Homework will be peer-reviewed. Each week, I will tell you another student in the class who will review your work, this person will be different each week. I will take the time spent reviewing into consideration when choosing how much work to assign.

You will give your homework to your reviewer, and receive a homework from another student. On Friday, you will return this student’s reviewed homework to me in class.

I STRONGLY encourage you to meet in one or more groups outside of class time to do the review collaboratively.

I will look over the reviewed homework and record numerical grades, then return the homeworks (hopefully on Monday).

Review rubric

When you are reviewing another student’s homework, you will not assign numerical grades. (I will do that after I collect the reviewed assignments.)

Write (the latin letters should be in ALL CAPITAL) next to each problem one of the following

  • GREAT: the problem is correct and there are no issues of clarity
  • GREAT \(-\varepsilon\): the problem is correct except for one or two small errors that don’t indicate a lack of understanding (eg copying a line, but writing 2 instead of 3, accidentally changing a \(+\) to a \(-\)). There are no issues of clarity except for those introduced by the small error. You should clearly mark the small error(s) justifying the \(-\varepsilon\).
  • UNCLEAR: the problem looks complete, but isn’t correct because of a mistake in methodology (an incorrect technique is used to solve the problem, or a correct technique is used incorrectly). You should write a comment about what you think was attempted and a hint toward a better solution (“try … when you see …”).
  • INCOMPLETE: the problem is not finished in a major way.
  • MISSING: the problem was assigned but there is no solution, or there is only the stub of a solution.

Quizzes

Quizzes of 2 or 3 questions will happen each week (during the first 10 minutes of class) to check that you’re keeping pace with the course. These are graded directly by me.

Exams

There will be one or two midterm exams (depending on the pace of the course). The exams have an in-class (50% of exam grade) and take-home (50% of exam grade) portion.

  • In-class: The in-class exam is designed to be completed within 60 minutes, without notes or technology
  • Take-home: The take-home exam is exactly the same as the in-class exam. You may use whatever resources you want, and may work in groups to complete it. However, you must write out your own solutions and may not copy a solution word-for-word from someone else.
